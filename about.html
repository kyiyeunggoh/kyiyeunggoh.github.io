About

I care about making technology useful when the stakes are real.

What keeps me interested in technology is what happens when it meets real life. A model can look good on paper and still fail in operations. A useful product can still leave people behind if they cannot understand it, access it or trust it.

I started my career closer to policy and operations because I was interested in how large systems shape people’s lives. Over time, I found myself wanting to get closer to implementation: not just deciding what should happen, but helping build the product, process and infrastructure that determine whether it actually does.

That shift has taken me from crisis response and digital capability work into civic technology and, today, national AI-enabled trust and safety products. The problems have changed, but the question I keep coming back to is the same: does this make the system work better for the person on the other side?

For me, impact is not only about scale. It is also about whether technology makes something safer, clearer or more accessible for people who might otherwise be left out.

That is also why I founded DatastorySG. As AI becomes easier to use and more powerful, I do not want the benefits to accrue only to people who already know how to navigate technology. Since 2019, we have reached close to 900 students, helping young people learn not just how to use data and AI, but how to question it, build with it and use it with judgment.

As AI becomes more capable, I think the product and policy questions become harder to separate. How do we make useful systems safer? How do we respond when the same capabilities can be used for abuse? How do we make sure new technology improves people’s lives rather than simply adding another layer of complexity?
