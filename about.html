About

My career has been about building technology for problems that matter in the real world.

I started in policy and operations, working on complex public problems where technology was increasingly part of the solution. Over time, I moved closer to building: launching 0 → 1 civic-tech products, helping lay the foundations for digital and AI capability at scale, and now leading national AI-enabled systems that detect and disrupt scams.

The common thread is translating complex problems into systems that work, combining product, policy, data and operations, and taking ideas from early concepts through to scaled deployment.

I’m particularly interested in what happens when technology begins to shape behaviour, safety and institutions at scale. That is what has drawn me increasingly towards AI, Trust & Safety, fraud and risk, and the policy and governance questions created by increasingly capable AI systems.

Looking ahead

Ambitious AI problems with real-world impact.

I’m interested in AI product, Trust & Safety, fraud and risk, AI policy and governance, safeguards, and other areas where technical systems interact directly with people and society. I’m especially drawn to roles where product and policy cannot be separated cleanly, where understanding how a system can be used, misused and governed is part of building the product well.

Outside work

I run DatastorySG, a pro-bono initiative I founded to help young people learn data and AI. Since its founding, we have reached close to 900 students, focusing not just on how to use new tools, but how to use them safely, responsibly and with judgment. It reflects a belief that runs through much of my work: technology should create meaningful impact, and we should make sure people are equipped to benefit from it rather than be left behind.

I’d love to connect with people working on consequential AI systems, policy and social-impact technology. LinkedIn is the easiest place to reach me; you can also explore what I build on GitHub or email me directly.
